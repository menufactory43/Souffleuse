AI autocomplete in every Mac app: how to get system-wide ghost text

Your Mac can already guess a word here and there. What you're probably looking for is something else: the rest of the sentence appearing in grey under your cursor — in Mail, in Slack, in your browser, everywhere — with one key to accept it. Here's why macOS doesn't do that out of the box, and how to add it.

Why macOS autocomplete only gets you 1–2 words

Since macOS Sonoma, Apple ships inline predictive text: as you type, the system proposes the next word or two in grey, and the space bar accepts them. It's genuinely useful — and genuinely limited. It predicts one or two words at a time, not sentences. It only appears in apps that use Apple's standard text system, so plenty of everyday writing surfaces — many Electron apps, some browsers, most web text areas — never show it. And its suggestions lean heavily toward English.

So the honest answer to "does my Mac have autocomplete?" is: yes, a small one, sometimes. If you write a lot — email, chat, notes, tickets — you quickly want the fuller version: whole phrases, in every app, in the language you actually type.

How system-wide ghost text works

A handful of Mac utilities solve this with the macOS Accessibility API — the same mechanism screen readers use to know what's on screen. The tool reads the text field you're typing in, asks a language model for a plausible continuation, and draws it in grey right at your caret. Because the Accessibility API works across applications, the same ghost text shows up in Mail, Notes, Messages, Slack, Safari or Chrome, Pages, Bear, Obsidian… anywhere there's a standard text field. Cotypist pioneered this category on the Mac; several tools now offer it, with different pricing and privacy models.

One nice consequence of this design: secure fields are excluded by construction. macOS marks password and credit-card fields as secure, and an Accessibility-based tool simply cannot read them — so nothing ever gets suggested there.

Step by step: autocomplete everywhere with Souffleuse

Souffleuse is one of these system-wide tools — a small menu-bar app whose language model runs entirely on your Mac. Setting it up takes a few minutes:

  1. Download Souffleuse (free during the beta, ~7.5 MB, notarized by Apple) and drag it into Applications. It lives in the menu bar.
  2. Grant the Accessibility permission when macOS asks — that's how it can see the text field you're writing in and place the ghost text at your caret. On first launch it downloads its language model once (0.4–1.5 GB depending on the model); that is the only network connection it ever makes.
  3. Open Mail, Slack, Notes — anything — and just type. When Souffleuse has a good guess at how your sentence continues, it appears in grey under your cursor.
  4. Press Tab to accept (word by word or in chunks), Esc to dismiss — or simply keep typing and the suggestion fades on its own.

Frequently asked questions

Does AI autocomplete work in every Mac app?

Tools built on the macOS Accessibility API work in almost every app that uses standard text fields: Mail, Notes, Messages, Slack, Safari or Chrome, Pages, Bear, Obsidian and more. Password and credit-card fields are ignored by design, because macOS marks them as secure.

Is my text sent to a server for the suggestions?

Not with Souffleuse. The language model runs on your Mac's Apple Silicon chip. The only connection it ever makes is the one-time model download at setup; after that it works entirely offline. No account, no telemetry.

Does it suggest in English?

Yes. Souffleuse is made in France and its interface is in French, but the suggestions follow whatever language you type — English, French and others work naturally.
